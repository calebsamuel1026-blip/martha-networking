# What the data said

Aggregate numbers from the system this repo was built from: one student's first week of cold networking emails in the fall, mostly to investment bankers and wealth advisors. No names, firms or reply text. Treat these as a strong starting point, not a law. Your own numbers (`/results`) win once you have enough of them.

**Evidence key.** A **real reply** is something a recipient actually wrote (paraphrased here). An **early signal** is a rate gap with a small sample. `tools/results.py` promotes a pattern to a lesson only at n >= 30 and z >= 2.

## Headline numbers

| What | Number |
|---|---|
| First emails delivered | 244 |
| Replied (any human reply) | 9.4% |
| Investment bankers who replied | 4.6% |
| Wealth advisors who replied | 18% |
| Banker replies that became a call or a referral | 4 (3 calls, 1 handoff to the head of analyst recruiting) |
| Time to reply | every human reply came within 43 hours |
| Bounce rate, pattern-guessed addresses | 4 of 30 (13%) |
| Bounce rate, addresses printed on a public page | 1 of 191 (0.5%) |

## What it means

**Speed.** Every reply came within 43 hours. After 4 days of silence, treat it as no reply. Follow-up 1 at day 7, follow-up 2 at day 14, then stop. (STRATEGY.md)

**Addresses.** Guessed addresses bounced about 25 times as often as printed ones. Only send to addresses printed on a live page and re-checked within 3 days. (RESEARCH.md)

**Who answers.** Advisors answered about four times as often as bankers. Bankers answered less, but their replies turned into calls and referrals. Aim at the people in your target group anyway, and expect a lower rate from the busiest seniors. *(early signal for the segment gap, real replies for the outcomes)*

**Hooks of theirs win.** All four banker calls and referrals opened on one precise thing the banker said, wrote or did. Emails that framed the student's interest in a sector with no hook of theirs went **0 of 9**. *(real replies + early signal)*

**Mentioning your own work is fine, once.** Banker emails that named the student's own company in one clause went 3 of 74. Without it, 1 of 41. No real difference, and 2 of the 3 banker calls named it in one plain clause tied to the banker's topic. An earlier count said these went 0 for 33, but it only matched one phrasing. Counting every phrasing corrected it. Lesson: count with broad patterns that catch every way something was said. *(early signal, corrected)*

**Firms that don't hire students say so.** Small firms asked about student roles: 4 of 4 human replies were some form of "we don't take interns". Confirm a firm hires students before emailing. *(real replies)*

## What real replies taught (paraphrased)

- **Credit the right person.** A managing director replied that the deal in our email was led by another group and he was second chair. He still took the call. Write "your team's work on" unless the page says they led it.
- **Hooks must be live.** An advisor replied that the group listed on her profile never got off the ground and she just hadn't removed it. Use dated, current things.
- **Make the ask plain.** An advisor replied asking the student to be direct about what he wanted. Answer with exactly one line: a short call to hear how they got into the field.
- **A handoff is a win.** A managing director copied the firm's head of analyst recruiting. Another senior banker said to send the résumé on a specific date. Thank the senior person in one line, write the new person directly, and follow up on any date they named.
- **Assistants run calendars.** Twice a banker handed scheduling to an assistant. Reply-all, thank the banker in a few words, give the assistant the logistics.

## What the user's own rulings added (not statistics, but they shaped everything)

- Never mention the application in a first email. It shows the real intention.
- No selling, no expertise claims, no shrinking words about your own work.
- Availability as one simple range. A three-window reply was "too many options".
- Warm replies mirror the other person's register. Narrated reactions ("that made me laugh") read as robotic.
- Facts about the student come only from what the student wrote down. One invented detail about the student's job reached four real inboxes before it was caught.

## What the data could not show yet

Subject-line style, send time, email length within the 110 to 160 range, and the question-mark rule all had too few sends per variant to call. `/results` will tell you once your own counts reach n >= 30 per variant.
