# Winning emails (anonymized)

Six real first emails that got calls or referrals, rewritten so nobody can be identified. Every person, firm, deal, school, town and fact is made up. The sender in all six is **Riley**, a fictional finance sophomore at the fictional Calder State University. What is kept from the originals: the order of the parts, the sentence shapes, the length, the subject format and the result.

Four went to investment bankers and two to wealth advisors. In any field, mirror these: the opening, the one precise thing of theirs, the one plain tie-in sentence, the "why you" line, the skeleton close and the subject. Only the hook and the field change.

Three of the six used a question mark. They were sent before the no-question rule (a question followed by a call ask makes the call sound like it is only for that question). Each one has a "today's version" line. Use that version.

## 1. Marcus Hale, Northgate Partners (157 words)

**Who:** Investment banker: managing director, distribution and logistics group. **Result:** Replied the same day asking for times. Call booked.

```text
Subject: Vintage Motorcycles | Northgate Interest

Hi Marcus,

Hope your week has been going well. According to your Northgate page, you rebuild vintage motorcycles in your spare time, and your 2025 piece for a grocery trade group said electric refrigerated vans are now reaching small delivery fleets. Together, those are the reason I'm writing. With the delivery app I started for local bakeries, what it costs to keep a van on the road is something I think about constantly, and those vans are about to change that math for small shops.

You see vehicles from the garage and from the deal table, and I can't think of a better person to hear from about where small-fleet delivery is heading.

I'm a current sophomore at Calder State University with a strong interest in investment banking at Northgate Partners. I would love to hop on a call with you and hear where you think it's all heading. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
Riley
```

**Why it worked**
- Two precise things from one person (a bio line and a dated article), joined by one plain clause about the student's own work on the same topic.
- The why-you line says exactly what makes him the right person: he sees vehicles from the garage and from the deal table.

## 2. Priya Sandoval, Harborline Capital (152 words)

**Who:** Investment banker: managing director, co-head of consumer. **Result:** Call booked through her assistant. Her reply also corrected who led the deal (see the note).

```text
Subject: Redline Freight | Harborline Interest

Hi Priya,

Hope your week has been going well. Your name on Harborline's sale of Redline Freight caught my eye since most of your work is in consumer. Delivery is where I started my company, a routing platform for local bakeries, so seeing a consumer banker on a freight deal is something I'd like to understand better. I also listened to your episode of the Middle Market Desk podcast, and your point that buyers now want much deeper diligence changed how I think about what an owner should be doing years before a sale.

Covering both consumer and freight gives you a view of deals I'd really like to learn from.

I'm a current sophomore at Calder State University with a strong interest in investment banking at Harborline Capital. If you have time in the coming weeks, I'd be grateful for a quick call. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
Riley
```

**Why it worked**
- A deal with her name on it that sits outside her usual sector is a natural, specific curiosity, and one point from her podcast proves the student actually listened.
- Note: the original said "seeing a consumer banker lead a freight deal". She replied that a different group led it and she was second chair. Write "your name on" or "your team's work on" unless the page says they led it. (Fixed above.)

## 3. Daniel Okoye, Westbrook & Lane (119 words)

**Who:** Investment banker: partner, co-head of distribution. **Result:** Replied in about 11 hours. Call booked through his assistant.

```text
Subject: Born in Krakow | Westbrook Interest

Hi Daniel,

Hope your week has been going well. In the scholarship profile you wrote years ago, you said you wanted an international MBA so you could help connect businesses across cultures. I was born in Poland and I speak Polish and conversational German, so working across cultures is something I care about too.

I'm a current sophomore at Calder State University with a strong interest in investment banking at Westbrook & Lane. Has that idea shown up in your deals, like the Tristar and Bellmont merger listed on your Westbrook page?

I would love to hop on a call with you about how your career has played out since then. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
Riley
```

**Why it worked**
- A less obvious find from deep research: an old profile in his own words, paraphrased with "years ago" because it was old, matched to one true fact about the student.
- Today's version of the question line (no question marks in first emails): "I'd love to hear whether that idea has shown up in your deals, like the Tristar and Bellmont merger listed on your Westbrook page."

## 4. Rohan Malhotra, Harborline Capital (148 words)

**Who:** Investment banker: managing director, global head of business services. **Result:** He copied the firm's head of analyst recruiting into the thread. That handoff became a call with recruiting.

```text
Subject: Multi-Decade Infrastructure | Harborline Interest

Hi Rohan,

Hope your week has been going well. Harborline's perspective from last summer calling water infrastructure a multi-decade investment opportunity caught my attention, mostly because of the time frame. Most of what I've done so far, from dispatch shifts to a company of my own, moves week to week, so a sector where the case is measured in decades is a way of thinking I'd really like to understand.

You also lead business services globally and have been at Harborline since your first years in banking, so you've watched the firm build out both of those practices. That long view is why I hoped to reach you.

I'm a current sophomore at Calder State University with a strong interest in investment banking at Harborline. I'd be grateful for a quick call whenever your schedule allows in the coming weeks. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
Riley
```

**Why it worked**
- The hook is the firm's own dated view, and the tie-in is a contrast (week to week versus decades) instead of a forced overlap.
- The why-you line uses his role and tenure as the reason to talk to him, not as a title read back to him.

## 6. Evan Brooks, Larkspur Wealth Group (114 words)

**Who:** Wealth advisor: vice president, financial advisor. **Result:** Replied the same day wanting a call. Call booked.

```text
Subject: Calder State and Sailing | Larkspur Interest

Hi Evan,

Fellow Hawk here, from a little farther up the lakeshore. I grew up in Port Alder, and your Larkspur page says you were raised in Millbrook. It also says many of your clients work in the marine trades and that you hold a captain's license yourself.

I'm a current sophomore at Calder State University with a strong interest in financial advising at Larkspur Wealth Group. Which came first for you, the boat or the marine clients?

I would love to hop on a call with you to find out how you went from racing sailboats in the junior nationals at 16 to financial planning. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
Riley
```

**Why it worked**
- Identity first: the same school (shown on his own page) and nearby hometowns, then two vivid facts from his page. The ask is about his unusual path, a story people enjoy telling.
- Today's version of the question line: "I'm curious which came first for you, the boat or the marine clients."

## What all six share

- **The opener is plain:** "Hope your week has been going well." (or a close variant), or a verified identity line ("Fellow Hawk here").
- **One precise thing of theirs,** named the way their own page or work names it, with a date or a source.
- **One plain sentence about the student,** true and tied to that thing. Never a list, never a number about themselves.
- **A reason it's this person,** either its own line (1, 2, 4) or folded into the ask (3, 5, 6).
- **The same skeleton close:** "I'm a current sophomore at <School> with a strong interest in <field> at <Firm>." Then the call ask, "Resume attached.", "Thanks for your time."
- **114 to 157 words.** Subjects of 2 to 4 words naming the thread, then " | <Firm> Interest".
- **Nothing about the application,** the program or recruiting. That came later, on the call.

## How to use these

1. Pick the winner whose hook type matches your research: a hobby or bio line (1), a deal or project outside their usual lane (2), something they wrote years ago (3), their firm's published view plus their role (4), a shared interest on their page (5), a shared school or hometown (6).
2. Keep its shape and its sentence rhythm. Swap in your recipient's facts and your own facts from `me/profile.md`.
3. Change the opener and the call clause if the batch already uses them a lot (PLAYBOOK.md, batch mechanics).
4. Run the gate.
