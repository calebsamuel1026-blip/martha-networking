# Replying

A reply means a person read your email and chose to answer. Answer the same day, inside their working hours. Draft it, show the user, send only on their "send".

## The voice: mirror their register

Match their length, looseness, greeting, sign-off and punctuation. Three loose lines get three loose lines back. A formal paragraph gets a short, polished paragraph. "You bet!" either ends the thread or gets one line.

**React, don't narrate a reaction.** These describe a feeling instead of having it, and they read as AI: "that made me laugh", "made me smile", "I love that", "great question", "great story", "so true", "thanks for sharing", "really resonated", "that's a great way to...". Also no "absolutely", no "totally!", and at most one "!" (only if they used one first).

**Don't grade them.** Never praise how they told or asked something ("great way to show", "perfect example of"). A student grading a 20-year veteran reads upside down.

**Friendly means specific and light.** Riff on one concrete detail they gave, the way you would with a friend.

> They wrote (a consultant, loose): "Ha, first year I flew to Omaha at 6am for a 20 minute meeting... the client cancelled when I landed."
>
> **Upright:** "That story made me laugh. It's a great way to show what client work is really like. Thanks so much for sharing."
>
> **Chill:** "Ha, landing in Omaha to a cancelled meeting. Bet you knew that airport well by the end of the year."

Smell test before it goes: read it out loud. If you wouldn't text it to a friend, rewrite it. Did you describe your reaction or grade theirs? Cut that clause and say the thing that made you react.

## Scheduling

**They ask for your availability.** Give ONE simple range from `me/availability.json`, plus at most one short exception if a real conflict sits inside it.

> Thanks, Dana. Mornings next week work for me any day, other than Wednesday.

- Never list day-by-day windows or class times. A three-window version was rejected as "too many options".
- Use their time zone if it differs from yours, and say which ("mornings Eastern").
- No booking links in a reply to a person who asked for times.

**They propose a time.** Confirm it and say you look forward to it. Two sentences, at most.

> Thursday at 10am Eastern works for me. Looking forward to it.

No recap of their email, no compliments, no backup options.

**An assistant is scheduling.** Reply-all. Thank the person in a few words, then give the assistant the logistics.

> Thank you, Marcus.
>
> Hi Jamie, mornings next week work for me any day. Happy to go with whatever fits Marcus's calendar.

**Logistics to settle in the thread:** who calls whom, which number or link, and the time zone. If the invite never arrives, send a short confirmation the day before.

## Referrals and handoffs

A handoff is a win. One of the four banker results in the source data was a managing director copying the firm's head of analyst recruiting into the thread.

**They copied someone in.** Reply-all once:
1. Thank the senior person in one line and move them to bcc ("Moving Marcus to bcc to spare his inbox.").
2. Then write to the new person: who you are in one line, what you'd like (a short call), when you're free (one range).

> Thank you for the introduction, Marcus (moving you to bcc).
>
> Hi Jamie, nice to meet you. I'm a sophomore at Lakeshore State, and Marcus mentioned you're the right person to talk to about how the firm gets to know students. I'd be grateful for a quick call. Mornings any day next week work for me.

**They named someone but didn't copy them.** Thank them in one line. Then write the new person a fresh email that names who sent you ("Marcus Hale suggested I reach out"). The address must be printed on a public page or given to you in the thread. If you only have a name, research a published address. Never guess one. If the firm is already at its cap, tell the user it is a referral so they can raise the cap for that firm on purpose.

**They named a date** ("send your résumé in early December"). Thank them, put the date on the calendar, follow up on that date, and stop until then.

## Declines

Gracious, one line, no re-ask, no "would you know anyone else".

> Thanks for letting me know, Dana, and for the quick reply.

Then label it `declined` and never follow up. If the reason is "we don't hire students", note the firm in `me/strategy.md` so nobody else there gets emailed.

If they ask you to stop or not to contact them, draft nothing. Label it `negative` with a note and never email them again. If a reply mentions lawyers, complaints or spam reports, draft nothing and hand it to the user.

## Warm replies

**Answer what they asked, in the first line.** Then the next step.

**Never restate the résumé.** They have it. If they ask why you're interested, give your real motivation in 2 or 3 sentences, then turn a question back to them. At most one detail about your experience, and only if it directly answers their question.

**"Please be direct, what can I help with?"** Answer with exactly the ask, in one line.

> Thanks, Kelsey. I'd love a short call to hear how you got into this work.

**Out-of-office** is not a reply. If it gives a return date, the follow-up clock starts from that date.

## Thank-you after a call

Send within 24 hours. 60 to 110 words.

1. Thanks for the time, naming the day.
2. **One specific thing they said,** in their words, and what you'll do with it.
3. If they offered an introduction, a one-line nudge. If the call ended with "may I send a note when I apply" and they said yes, say you will.

> Hi Marcus,
>
> Thank you for the time on Friday. Your point that first-year associates who ask for the messy files early learn the most stuck with me, and I'm going to start doing that with our club's books this semester.
>
> If Jamie is still the right person to talk to about student events, I'd be grateful for that introduction. I'll send you a short note when I apply.
>
> Best,
> Sam

No new pitch, no résumé, no list of everything you learned.

## Every reply, before it goes
- [ ] Answers what they actually asked
- [ ] Matches their length and tone
- [ ] No narrated reactions, no grading, no résumé restated
- [ ] One clear next step
- [ ] Availability as one simple range, never class times
- [ ] Drafted only. Sent on the user's "send".
