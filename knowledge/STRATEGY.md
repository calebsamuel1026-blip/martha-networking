# Strategy: network backward from application dates

The point of networking is not to be liked. It is to have someone at the firm **who knows your name before your application lands**, so it gets read, flagged or passed along. Everything below serves that.

## 1. Work backward from the date

1. List every application the user is eligible for, with its opening date (`me/strategy.md`). Programs for the user's class year, early-insight or sophomore events that feed them, and open seats for the next summer.
2. Start networking at a firm **3 to 8 weeks before its window opens.** Earlier and they forget you. Later and the application is in before the call happens.
3. Group firms into waves by window:

| Wave | When to network | Firms |
|---|---|---|
| 1: open now or within a month | this week | <firms with windows open or opening soon> |
| 2: opens in 1 to 3 months | starting now, spread out | <firms> |
| 3: opens in 3 to 8 months | later | <firms> |

4. **Apply within 48 hours of a window opening.** Many applications are read on a rolling basis.
5. After applying, stop new cold outreach at that firm and send the update email (section 4).

Rough timing patterns (always confirm on the firm's own careers page or on a call):
- **Investment banking, consulting, Big 4:** many summer programs open 9 to 12 months before the summer, and sophomore or early-insight events often open in the fall or winter before.
- **Tech:** most internship postings open in late summer and fall for the next summer, and fill on a rolling basis.
- **Marketing, engineering, healthcare, law and smaller firms:** often closer to the summer (winter and spring), with more variation.

## 2. Per-firm plan

- **3 people per firm, ever:** 1 recruiter or campus lead, plus 2 people in the group the user would join. Alumni first. (RESEARCH.md.)
- Move to the next person only after **7 days** with no reply.
- If someone at the firm already has a call booked with the user, hold new emails to their teammates until after that call. Put a "send after <date>" note on those drafts.

## 3. The three asks on every call

The first email never mentions the application. The call does, at the end, after the questions about them:

- **(a) Timing.** "When does the application for my class year open at <Firm>, and how does the <group> get to know students before then?"
- **(b) A flag or a next person.** "When I apply, would you be comfortable flagging my application, or is there someone in the group or in recruiting I should talk to next?"
- **(c) Permission to update.** "May I send you a short note when I apply?"

These go last, on every call, in this order. CALL-PREP.md puts them on the sheet.

## 4. The update email after applying

When the user applies, every contact at that firm gets a short note. Write it the way the user would actually say it. The default, in plain words:

> Hi <First name>,
>
> I wanted to update you and let you know I applied for the <exact position title> position. Thanks again for your time <on our call / last month>.
>
> Best,
> <First name>

- Send it to everyone at that firm: people who took a call, people who replied, and people who never answered. For the silent ones it doubles as a follow-up, so drop the "thanks again" line.
- Use the exact title from the posting. Reply in the original thread (`reply_to`).
- One or two lines. No résumé recap, no ask beyond what they already offered. If they offered to flag it, you can add: "If you're still comfortable flagging it, I'd be grateful."
- Run it with `/update-after-apply <firm>`.

## 5. Follow-up timing

From the source data: **every human reply came within 43 hours.** So:

- **4 days of silence = no reply.** Don't wait longer than that to treat it as one.
- **Follow-up 1 at day 7** after the first email. **Follow-up 2 at day 14.** Then stop for good.
- Each follow-up: reply in the same thread, shorter than the first email, add **one new small real thing** (a fresh finding about their work, a dated development at their firm, a short line on why them), then restate the call ask plainly.
- No guilt and no filler: never "just bumping", "circling back", "in case you missed it", "following up on my last email".
- No résumé restated. They have it.
- If someone gives a date ("send your résumé in early December"), follow up on that date and stop until then.
- An out-of-office is not a reply. If it gives a return date, count the days from the return.
- A decline ends it. No follow-ups after a no.

## 6. Keep the calls you booked

Never cancel a call that is already on the calendar, even if the firm has dropped off the user's list. Cancelling burns a bridge with someone who said yes. Use the call: ask the three asks, and if the firm has a group closer to the user's target, ask for an introduction there.

If the user truly can't make it, reschedule once with one simple range ("Any morning next week works for me"), never ghost.

## 7. Keep in touch

- Thank-you within 24 hours of every call (REPLIES.md).
- If they gave advice and the user acted on it, a short note 3 to 4 weeks later saying what they did. No ask.
- Every 4 to 6 months, one line of real news tied to their advice. Never "just checking in".
- Log every touch against the firm's window, so the right note goes out when the application opens.

## 8. A good week

- Every morning: replies first (`/check-replies`), then follow-ups due (`/follow-ups`).
- Then new outreach for the nearest wave: research, draft, read 3 samples, send.
- Calls: prep the day before (`/call-prep`), thank-you within 24 hours.
- Weekly: `/results` to see what's working.
