# How first emails are written

This is the shape that earned every call and referral in the source system: 4 calls or referrals from investment bankers and replies from 18% of wealth advisors (see RESULTS.md). It works in any field because only two things change from email to email: **the hook** (one precise thing of theirs) and **the field** in your interest line.

Read WINNING-EMAILS.md before drafting. Copy its opening, tie-in, "why you" line, skeleton and subject format. Only the hook changes.

## The shape

```
Subject: <2-3 words naming their thing> | <Firm> Interest

Hi <First name>,

Hope your week has been going well. <One precise thing of theirs, with where and when.> <One plain sentence tying it to you.>

<Short "why you" line.>

I'm a current <year> at <School> with a strong interest in <field> at <Firm>. I would love to hop on a call with you and hear <about their thing or their path>. Resume attached.

Thanks for your time.

Best,
<Your first name>
```

**Length: 119 to 159 words** in the body (the range of the 4 banker winners). The 2 advisor winners ran 114 and 118, so 110 is the floor. Count, don't guess.

**Format:** each paragraph is one unbroken line with a blank line between paragraphs. No bullets, bold, links or long signature blocks.

## Each part

**1. Opening line.** "Hope your week has been going well." It is short and human. If two people in the same batch share a first name, give one of them a different opener (the gate reads "Hi Chris, Hope your week..." as one sentence and flags it): "Hope you've had a good week so far." / "Hope October has been treating you well."

**2. One precise thing of theirs.** Something they said, wrote or worked on, named exactly, with where and when: a line from their bio, a dated article or talk, a deal or project on their page, a podcast point. Two facets of the same person are fine (a bio line plus their 2025 article). Two unrelated hooks are not.
Rotate how you bring it in. "Your <Firm> page says" crept into most openers once and read like a template. Mix in: "I saw on <Firm>'s site...", "Your bio mentions...", "In your 2025 talk at...", "...was the first thing I noticed on your bio", or lead with the fact itself ("Before <Firm>, you were...").

**3. One plain tie-in sentence.** Say how it connects to you, once, in plain words. It can be your background ("I was born in Poland and I speak Polish, so working across cultures is something I care about too.") or your own work tied to their topic ("With the delivery app I started for local bakeries, what it costs to keep a van on the road is something I think about constantly."). Transitions must feel natural, never like a checklist.

**4. Why you.** One short line saying why this person, not just this firm:
- "I can't think of a better person to hear from about where <topic> is heading."
- "That long view is why I hoped to reach you."
- "Covering both <A> and <B> gives you a view of <deals/projects> I'd really like to learn from."

**5. Skeleton close.** Keep these sentences. They are allowed to repeat across emails. Don't chop them into fragments to dodge repetition.
- "I'm a current <year> at <School> with a strong interest in <field> at <Firm>." Name the **field**, not a niche ("investment banking at <Firm>", not "industrials M&A at <Firm>"). Never open this line with "I'm reaching out".
- The ask: "I would love to hop on a call with you and hear..." or "If you have time in the coming weeks, I'd be grateful for a quick call." A call, never "15 minutes", never an email reply.
- "Resume attached." / "Thanks for your time." / "Best," / first name.

**6. Subject.** 2 or 3 words naming their thing, then " | <Firm> Interest". Examples: "Vintage Motorcycles | Northgate Interest", "Multi-Decade Infrastructure | Harborline Interest", "First-Year Audits | Ridgeway & Cole Interest". Use the firm's short name. No ask, no "Re:", no "Quick question".

### The interest line by field

| Field | Interest line | "The group you'd apply to" means |
|---|---|---|
| Investment banking | a strong interest in investment banking at <Firm> | the industry or product group (healthcare, industrials, restructuring) in one office |
| Accounting / Big 4 | a strong interest in audit at <Firm> (or tax, or advisory) | the service line in one office, ideally the industry practice inside it (audit for real estate clients in that office) |
| Consulting | a strong interest in consulting at <Firm> | the office you'd join (most firms hire by office), then a practice or industry area |
| Tech | a strong interest in software engineering at <Firm> (or product management, data science) | the product area or team (payments, infrastructure) |
| Marketing | a strong interest in brand marketing at <Firm> | the brand or function team (a product line's brand team, growth marketing) |
| Engineering | a strong interest in civil engineering at <Firm> | the division, site or project group |
| Healthcare | a strong interest in healthcare administration at <Org> | the department, unit or fellowship |
| Law (pre-law) | a strong interest in corporate law at <Firm> | the practice group in one office |
| Wealth management | a strong interest in financial advising at <Firm> | the advisor's team or branch |

The user's own line lives in `me/profile.md` as `interest_line`. Use it word for word.

## Hard rules

1. **No semicolons, em dashes or en dashes.** Plain sentences.
2. **No question marks in a first email.** A question followed by a call ask makes the call sound like it is only for that question. State curiosity instead: "I'd love to hear how that idea has shown up in your deals."
3. **Never mention the application, recruiting, an internship, a program, a posted role or hiring in a first email.** It shows the real intention and turns a conversation into a transaction. The application lives in the draft's `application` field. It comes up on the call (STRATEGY.md, the three asks) and in the update email after you apply.
4. **Your own work: once, plainly, tied to their topic.** One clause about your job, project, club or company is fine (2 of 3 banker calls named the student's company in one plain clause). Never its features, numbers, users, revenue, GPA, awards or a list. One résumé item per email at most. The email is about them.
5. **No expertise or knowledge claims.** Banned: "I know best", "so I know", "firsthand", "I've seen how", "where I've built things", "I understand the challenges", "in my experience". Also no shrinking words on your own work: "small", "little", "tiny", "modest", "just a". Plain and confident, no adjectives either way.
6. **No selling.** If it is obvious you are selling yourself, you won't sell. Don't restate the résumé they already have attached.
7. **No AI-tell phrases.** "I hope this email finds you well", "I came across your profile", "I'm reaching out", "passionate about", "leverage", "I was impressed by", "resonated", "that caught my eye because", "I'd love to pick your brain", "great question", "love that", "absolutely". No exclamation marks in first emails.
8. **Credit deals and work correctly.** Write "you led" only when the page names them as the lead. Otherwise "your team's work on X", "X on your deal list", "one of the deals on your group's page". A real banker reply corrected us: the deal had been led by a different group and he was second chair. He still took the call, but don't make him correct you.
9. **Hooks must be live and dated.** Use a 2025-26 deal, quote, article, talk or bio line. A side project or club on a bio needs a recent sign it still exists: one advisor told us the group on her profile never got off the ground. Anything older gets "years ago" and a paraphrase, never a quote presented as current.
10. **Never overstate them.** Their page says "lists sailing as an interest", so you don't write "avid sailor". Never infer heritage, religion, politics or family from a name, a photo or a focus area. A school tie counts only when a firm, school or alumni-org page shows it (a search snippet is not enough).
11. **One shared thread per email,** the closest match only. Rank threads: shared identity (same school, hometown, language, sport) beats shared path (founder, career switch), which beats shared topic. Don't stack two hobbies.
12. **No real tie, no email.** If nothing of theirs connects to anything true about you, pick another person at the firm.
13. **Don't announce their title back to them** ("You're a Partner on..."). Use their role only as the reason ("you've watched the firm build out both practices").
14. **Facts only from `me/profile.md` and sourced findings.** Never add color to the user's life ("grew up around small businesses") that they didn't write.
15. **Replies mirror their register.** Match their length, looseness and punctuation. React, don't narrate a reaction. See REPLIES.md.

### Batch mechanics (so the gate passes)
- The gate caps any 4-word phrase at 30% of a batch (`int(n*0.3)`, minimum 3): 3 emails in a batch of 10, 7 in a batch of 25. Your skeleton sentence and `interest_line` from `me/profile.md` are allowed to repeat.
- The gate also flags any sentence of 8+ words that appears word for word in two emails. Two people at the same firm in one batch would get the identical interest sentence, so vary the words around it for the second one: "As a current sophomore at <School>, I have a strong interest in <field> at <Firm>, and I'd be grateful for a quick call." Better still, put the second person at a firm in a later batch.
- Vary everything else: the opener, the way you bring in the hook, the call clause ("I would love to hop on a call..." / "I'd be grateful for a quick call...").
- Never work around the gate (no fake spellings, no deleting a placeholder you can't fill). If something is unknown, leave the `<placeholder>` in and tell the user what's missing.

## Three rewrites

### 1. A whole first email (Big 4 audit)

**BAD** (subject "Audit Internship Inquiry")
> Dear Ms. Reyes, I hope this email finds you well. I came across your profile and I'm reaching out because I'm applying to Ridgeway & Cole's summer leadership program. I'm passionate about accounting and I'm a hard worker with a 3.9 GPA, treasurer of two clubs, and payroll experience, so I know how important accuracy is. Do you have any advice for my application? I'd love to connect!

Wrong: AI-tell opener, names the program, lists the résumé, an expertise claim, a question, an exclamation, nothing of hers, a subject that names an ask.

**GOOD** (subject "First-Year Audits | Ridgeway & Cole Interest")
> Hi Dana,
>
> Hope your week has been going well. Your talk at the state CPA society's spring conference on first-year audits of newly public companies was the first thing I noticed on your bio, mostly the point that the first year sets the habits a client keeps for a decade. I keep the books for our club volleyball team, and closing our first full year this spring is when I started to see why the setup matters so much.
>
> Your bio says you've worked on first-year audits since your days as a senior, so I can't think of a better person to hear from about what makes them go well.
>
> I'm a current sophomore at Lakeshore State University with a strong interest in audit at Ridgeway & Cole. I would love to hop on a call with you and hear how you got into this work. Resume attached.
>
> Thanks for your time.
>
> Best,
> Sam

### 2. The one line about your own work (software engineering)

**BAD (selling):** "I built an app with 2,000 users that predicts bus arrivals with 94% accuracy, so I know firsthand how hard real-time data is."
**BAD (shrinking):** "I made a little bus app for my campus."
**GOOD:** "With the bus-arrival app I started for our campus, getting a phone to know which stop it's actually at is something I think about a lot." (Their hook was a 2025 talk on location accuracy in transit apps. One clause, tied to their topic, no numbers.)

### 3. The hook line (finance or consulting)

**BAD:** "Congrats on leading the sale of Brightwater Dental. I also saw you co-founded a mentoring group for first-gen students."
Wrong: the deal page lists her on the team, not as lead. The mentoring group's last sign of life is from 2019. Two hooks.
**GOOD:** "Brightwater Dental on your team's deal list caught my attention, mostly because it closed the same month as two other dental groups your firm advised this year."
One hook, credited correctly, dated, and specific enough that she knows you read the page.

## Before a draft goes to the gate
- [ ] One precise, sourced, live thing of theirs, credited correctly
- [ ] One plain tie-in sentence, true per `me/profile.md`
- [ ] A "why you" line
- [ ] Skeleton close with the exact interest line, call ask, "Resume attached."
- [ ] 119 to 159 words, no `;`, no dashes, no `?`, no `!`
- [ ] No application, program, recruiting or hiring words
- [ ] Subject: 2-3 words of theirs + " | <Firm> Interest"
- [ ] Read it out loud. If it sounds like a template or a cover letter, rewrite it.
